# Analytic Epidemiology: Topics Outline

The three teaching sessions are October 7, 12, and 14, 2026. October 5 is quiz-only.

## October 7: Experimental and Cohort Studies

1. **Association and causation:** brief review of the distinction and probabilistic causality.
2. **Experimental versus observational studies:** whether investigators assign exposure.
3. **Randomized controlled trials:** basic design, randomization, internal and external validity, baseline-table interpretation, strengths and limitations.
4. **Cohort studies:** basic design, prospective versus retrospective cohorts, when to use them, and the Framingham Heart Study.
5. **Bias and comparison groups:** loss to follow-up (attrition bias), exposure misclassification, confounding, and the healthy-worker effect.
6. **Measures of association:** calculating and interpreting risk, relative risk, and absolute risk difference.
7. **Practice:** passive-smoking and respiratory-infection calculations, interpretation polls, and a summary of cohort strengths and limitations.

## October 12: Case-Control Studies

1. **Design distinctions:** retrospective cohort versus case-control studies and situations where cohort studies are impractical.
2. **Case-control design:** selecting cases and controls, assessing prior exposure, and identifying the design.
3. **Smoking and lung cancer:** study-design discussion and the Doll and Hill example.
4. **Selecting controls:** target and source populations, the study-base principle, and the Shands Hospital exercise.
5. **Sources of bias:** selection, recall, interviewer, and observer bias.
6. **Odds ratios:** why they are used, calculation from a two-by-two table, interpretation, and the rare-disease approximation.
7. **Practice and comparison:** odds-ratio and bias polls, strengths and limitations, and cohort versus case-control comparisons.

## October 14: Ecologic Studies and Confounding

1. **Ecologic studies:** group-level analysis, ecological fallacy, strengths and limitations.
2. **Comparing designs:** dietary fat and breast cancer examples from an ecological study and a randomized trial.
3. **Bias review:** social desirability bias, the Hawthorne effect, identifying bias and behavior changes, strategies for reducing selection and information bias, and why larger samples do not generally eliminate bias.
4. **Confounding review:** the ice-cream/drowning example and the conventional three confounder criteria.
5. **Limitations of those criteria:** Hernán’s common-cause and collider examples, video, and questions about adjustment and causal structure.
6. **Addressing confounding:** randomization, identifying and measuring potential confounders, and cautious interpretation.
7. **Integration:** cohort versus case-control review, epidemiology wrap-up, and applying study design, measures, bias, and confounding to the final presentation.
